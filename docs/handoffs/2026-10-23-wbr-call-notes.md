# Partner call notes: WBR, Oct 23, 2026

Gemini meeting notes from the partners' weekly call on Oct 23, 2026, trimmed to the parts about the website, the brand and the reel. Client projects, pricing, proposals, leads, the email system, admin and personal conversation were left out on purpose; the full notes and transcript are in Google Drive. The summary of what was said is written from the transcript, because Gemini's own summary of this stretch is out of order in places.

## Gemini's quick notes (site and brand only)

* Proposed committing to a new brand positioning for 6 months to effectively gather performance analytics and feedback.
* Explored shifting the homepage tagline away from generic messaging toward a focus on handling difficult and high-stakes projects.
* Discussed refining website copy to feature concise taglines emphasizing bold choices rather than standard industry tropes.
* Committed to a brand differentiator centered on tackling difficult and high-risk projects for 1 year.
* Expressed the philosophy that lazy videos are worse than no content, rejecting safe corporate deliverables.
* Discussed the unorthodox filmmaking style of the documentary *You Can See Everything* as a creative benchmark.
* Proposed streamlining website navigation to minimize clutter and centralize core offerings across pages.
* Positioned the red pill site as a dedicated platform for industry manifestos and hot takes.

**Gemini's decisions (aligned):** brand identity focused on difficult projects, committed for one year. Simplified website structure: a few main pages, plus a separate manifesto area.

## What was said, from the transcript

**Commit to the position (00:40 to 01:00).** Chris asked to treat the position as a one-way door for about six months, so feedback and analytics mean something; a site that keeps changing teaches nothing. He said "We make films you feel" doesn't say much anymore because every company wants that, and that the site should say why we're better at the client's goal, not repeat what every vendor promises (his aspirin and Tylenol example). Ari agreed and said commit for a year. Tony: "we don't pass on difficult projects."

**The preview homepage (00:43 to 00:46, 01:12).** Only the homepage changed; the other pages come after it's locked. Ari likes how slim it is, thinks more words could come out, and suggested a reel in the gap under the hero. Chris wants as much creative as copy on the page, with the copy and the creative saying the same thing. Ari liked the order: work, What we believe, then contact.

**The headline (00:45 to 00:56).** Options raised, none chosen:

| Option | Who | Reason given |
| --- | --- | --- |
| We shoot the difficult ones. | Brief (Oct 21) | The current line |
| We shoot the difficult. | Tony, Ari | Drop "ones": redundant, leaves it open |
| We shoot the challenging. | Tony | "Difficult" can read as difficult people to work with |
| Make it bold. | Ari; Chris "doesn't hate it" | "Shoot" undersells that we produce end to end |

Ari also floated "impact"; Chris rejected it as a cliché every company uses. Chris said only the first line gets to be a slogan ("Just do it"), and the line under it has to say exactly what we mean: "Films about hard subjects and hard places for organizations that can't afford to get it wrong." Chris wants the brand itself to be bold, and wants us to read like special forces: the crew you can ask to do something crazy without being told no. Tony noted the upside: everyone thinks their own project is difficult, so the position invites everyone's job.

**"Based in Phoenix. We work anywhere." (00:52 to 00:53).** Ari: a stock line from the first AI draft of the site, and he'd rather say where we are in one place only, the contact page. Chris: it's common because it matters, and we need to read as able to work internationally. Not settled.

**How we earn it (00:59).** Chris: the copy should answer "what makes you qualified for that?", partly with the research. Ari handed the copy to Chris: copy is Chris's, formatting, design and the back end are Ari's.

**Wait for the film (01:03 to 01:07).** Ari and Tony suggested Chris watch *You Can See Everything* (Lance Oppenheim, A24) before finishing the copy, since it might change his view. Tony's case: a very difficult documentary shot in a way nobody expected, no sit-down interviews, cinema cameras where a documentary would normally use small ones. Chris: "We'll see." Ari: if it doesn't land, change a word later.

**Site structure (01:13 to 01:15).** Chris writes the copy and needs stills, clips and shots from his and Tony's drives; Ari offered to place them. Chris: Work and Case studies are really one page, and the site can be a homepage, a portfolio page and one other page. Tony agreed. Ari: no dropdowns, about five main pages, keep the back pages for search if they help, and ask Claude about that.

**The red pill (01:15 to 01:16).** Chris and Ari: the red pill becomes the separate site for the manifesto, hot takes and longer thinking about the work and the industry. The main site stays professional; the red pill is where people go to see what we're really about.

**The reel (01:16 to 01:20).** Tony will cut a new reel for the position and is already thinking about the visuals; he won't put copy in it that locks us to a line. Chris's two pillars for it: physically hard (a remote place, a high-speed or risky shot) and high-stakes (a heavy interview, a governor's office, brand risk). His example: a few seconds from a Your Voice, Your Power interview in a historic Senate chamber, cut against fast, technical footage.

**Tone (01:20 to 01:26).** Chris knows the position implies other crews are timid, and doesn't want to sound catty or contrarian for its own sake; he does believe a lazy film is worse than none. Ari prefers plain statements of how we work over "we do this, not that" contrasts. Tony: clients hire us for an idea they didn't think of, like showing the teleprompter on camera when non-actors read scripted lines.
