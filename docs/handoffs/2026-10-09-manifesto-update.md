# Update to the Thrill Wave manifesto site pass (Oct 9, 2026)

Follow-up to `2026-10-08-manifesto-site-pass-v0.md`, from Chris, Oct 9, 2026 (pasted into the Claude Code chat; saved here word for word on Oct 15). This supersedes v0 wherever the two disagree, and `2026-10-09-site-pass-v1-post-wbr.md` supersedes this one in turn. Positioning and copy direction only; design, nav, film curation and bios are off limits.

Since v0 we settled the mechanism, and it changes the spine of the manifesto. Thrill Wave sells a way of working, not a crew. The chain is: nothing changes anyone unless they feel something real; nothing real comes out unless someone goes and gets it; getting it is the hard part, meaning the research, the hard questions, the patience to sit with someone until they say the true thing, and the nerve to keep it in the cut. That is what Thrill Wave does. The camera comes last. Every page should make the hard part the thing we're known for, because every other shop promises impact and emotion and nobody sells the part where someone has to go and get the truth out of real people. SITREP is step one of that work (you can't ask the hard question without knowing the world), so keep describing it as research and a point of view, now framed as the start of finding the true thing rather than a standalone product.

Two rules follow from the mechanism and are now hard rules for all copy. First, no actors. Thrill Wave is a nonfiction house: real people telling real stories in their real lives. We don't direct performances and we don't make scripted drama. The drama is essential, but it comes from what actually happened to someone, never from casting. Second, we are not adding feeling, we are finding the feeling that was already there. Keep the "nevers" next to any claim about emotion so it can't read as tear-jerking: no narration, no actors, no stock, nothing telling the viewer what to feel. The chain says why we work this way; the nevers prove it.

The line that carries the business side of the position is this, and it goes in the manifesto word for word: "Blood, sweat and tears built this boring world." Every organization assumes its story is dull. Our position is that nobody's is, they just haven't let anyone look. Use that framing on Who we serve and anywhere the copy speaks to businesses, professional services, builders and makers. The proof for this claim is the boring-company films, not ITCA: Rumco, Citgo and Brundage Bone, Payroc. Dear Mom and the vaccine films remain the proof for the trust claim. Your Voice, Your Power and the Decadence aftermovies remain the pair that shows the method is an approach, not a genre.

The name now has a meaning and the copy can use it, lightly and without a lecture. A thrill wave is what goes through a person when something true hits them; "thrill" comes from an old word meaning to pierce. One line somewhere on About is plenty. Don't build a section around it.

Word rules, applying everywhere including docs/VOICE.md, the Tuesday Intel posts and llms.txt: "video production company" stays as the category phrase in titles, meta descriptions, the Google Business Profile and the first line of the homepage, because that's what people search; it is the only place "video" describes our own work. Everything we author is a "film": the docuseries, the aftermovie, the team film, the explainer. Derivatives are "cuts": vertical cuts, 30-second cuts, social cuts. Never stretch "film" over a 9:16 clip. We are "filmmakers" or "our crew", never "videographers". Avoid "audience" except where the copy is arguing against the idea; say "people" or "one person". No "content", no "campaign", no "agency", no AI in our process, no headcount, no em dashes, no exclamation points, and no explaining these word choices on the site; use them consistently and let readers notice. One new standing element: every film gets an end card that reads "A Thrill Wave film". That is the "we put our name on it" beat made literal. Note it in VOICE.md as a delivery rule even though the site itself doesn't render it.
