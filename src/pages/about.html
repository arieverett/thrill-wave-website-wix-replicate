About Thrill Wave · Est. 2018

Made of people, for people.

Thrill Wave is a video production company in Phoenix, Arizona. We go and get the true story out of real people, then make films that move anyone who sees them and still hold up years later.

Origin

Founded in 2018 in Phoenix, Arizona.

We started Thrill Wave because we love making things with people, and we kept watching the people get lost. Dated processes, smoke-and-mirrors pricing and endless bidding took the heart out of something that should be simple: good people, a true story and the care to tell it well. So we built the company we would want to hire, and we've tried to do right by everyone who walks through the door ever since. Here's where that has taken us so far:

  • 2018Founded in Phoenix
  • 10+Years behind the camera
  • 80+Organizations we've worked with
  • 12Fields and counting

Our founders came to film from very different places, and each path taught us something we still bring to set, from the rhythm of a good edit to the habit of asking why a story matters. Thrill Wave is where it all comes together. Even the name is the point: a thrill wave is what goes through you when something true hits, and “thrill” comes from an old word meaning to pierce.

Our manifesto

What we believe.

Here's what we believe about making films, and why it shapes every one we make:

There's no such thing as an audience. There are only people, and people take in a story in remarkably similar ways. You don't need to have lived someone's life to feel what they felt. It just has to be true about being human. So we don't make films for demographics. We make them for the part of people that's the same in everyone.

The camera is the easy part. Anyone can buy a cinema camera now. Nothing changes anyone unless they feel something real, and nothing real comes out unless someone goes and gets it. Getting it is the hard part: the research, the hard questions, the patience to sit with someone until they say the true thing, and the nerve to keep it in the cut. That's what we do. The camera comes last.

We don't direct actors. We get real people to say true things. We make nonfiction: real people telling real stories from their own lives. The drama matters, but it comes from what actually happened to someone, never from casting. Point a lens at a person and they start performing, and getting past that takes time, so we'd rather wait an hour for one true sentence than film twenty polished ones. We don't add feeling, we find the feeling that was already there, so there's no narration, no stock footage and nothing telling you what to feel.

Blood, sweat and tears built this boring world. Every organization assumes its story is dull. Nobody's is. They just haven't let anyone look. Behind every payment that clears and every slab of concrete that gets poured, someone took a risk, a crew showed up early and somebody's day went better because of it. We've gone and looked for Rumco, for Payroc and for Citgo and Brundage Bone, and the story was there every time.

Nobody watches homework. People argue with a lecture and get lost in a story. Most films that institutions make set out to explain something, so people sit through them looking for reasons to disagree, or don't sit through them at all. When a brief asks us to teach, we look for the person the lesson happened to. A series on how government works became ten episodes about the people government touches, and our client trusted us to make it that way.

We take a side, and we tell you whose. There's no such thing as a neutral film. Every cut is a choice about what stays and what goes, and a film that claims no point of view is hiding its author. We'd rather show you ours.

We'll argue for the good version. Every project has a safe version and a good version, and the safe one is the risky one. It follows the trend, copies what a peer made and aims at a demographic, so it starts to date the day it comes out. The good one is built on something true about people, so it keeps working for anyone who sees it, years after launch. That makes timeless art the responsible way to spend money on a film, not the indulgent one. We'll push for the good version every time, and we'll tell you why. If you want exactly what you asked for, we're probably the wrong crew.

The best night of someone's year counts too. Some people wait all year for one night at a festival. When we cut the aftermovie, we cut it for them, with the same care we give our most serious documentary, because to them it matters just as much.

Our name goes on it. You don't hire a few people with cameras. You get the Thrill Wave treatment: people who care what happens after the film goes out. We don't put our name on anything we don't mean.

Under all of it sits one promise: we protect the people we film. We ask for consent first and treat everyone with dignity, especially when the story is personal.

Our values

What we care about.

Those beliefs show up as a few habits, from what we shoot to what we cut and when we speak up. They're what you can count on from us on every project:

  1. 01

    We keep it true

    We report first, check our facts and let people speak for themselves. Everyone on screen is a real person with something real at stake. We don't stage what should be felt, and we don't promise what we can't film.

  2. 02

    We keep it simple

    Untangling a complicated subject is our job, not the viewer's, so the message reaches people clearly.

  3. 03

    We care about the craft

    We love the problem-solving on set, the light and the edit that makes it all click. If a cut doesn't move us, we don't expect it to move anyone else, so we keep working until it does.

  4. 04

    We stay accountable

    You'll know who's on your project before the first shoot day, and we answer for how it turns out.

  5. 05

    We talk straight

    We're honest about scope, cost and timeline from the start, so there are no surprises, upsells or overpromises later.

  6. 06

    We care what happens next

    The final cut isn't the finish line. We pay attention to where the film lands and what it does for the people who watch it.
